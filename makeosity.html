The operating company

A decade of building things that work in real classrooms.

Geppetto Industries is a venture of Makeosity, Inc. — a New York company that has spent ten years building STEM programs, training teachers, and inventing alongside students in the public schools that need it most.

Since 2016

Built inside the school system, not around it.

Makeosity holds a ten-year relationship with New York City Public Schools: a contract to develop STEM programs, deliver professional development to teachers, and provide in-school STEM coaching in the classroom. The work is measured where it's hardest to fake — in how students and teachers actually use it.

Over that decade, Makeosity has raised more than $10 million through New York State Education Department and foundation funding to put those programs into schools, with development partners including the Carnegie Mellon Robotics Academy.

Invention

US Patent 9,755,481 B2

Dr. Karen Kaun invented a patented mobile charging device together with students from New York City Public Schools. The complete prototype was brought to life in partnership with engineers from Autodesk and Olin College of Engineering.

Students as inventors · industry and engineering partners as collaborators.

The founder

Dr. Karen P. Kaun

Karen Kaun has spent twenty years doing one thing exceptionally well: taking a new idea from zero to multi-million-dollar scale, again and again, inside systems most people find impossible to move. She builds the thing, proves it works with hard evidence, and gets it funded.

At Makeosity she built FaceLab into a multi-million-dollar operation inside New York City Public Schools, reaching 26 schools, 3,000+ students, and 375 parents trained and paid as classroom robotics coaches, on more than $4.2M in public, corporate, and foundation funding, with external evaluation documenting measurable gains in student learning. Across her career she has won more than $10M from NYSED, the Fund for Public Schools, Microsoft, the W.K. Kellogg and William T. Grant Foundations, and federal research programs. She is the inventor on U.S. Patent 9,755,481 B2, taken from idea to issued patent.

Earlier, as co-founder of Knowledge iTrust, she built one of the earliest global e-learning networks and created Peace Diaries after 9/11, publishing student writing across 13+ countries in work covered by The New York Times and commended by President Bill Clinton. She holds a doctorate from Teachers College, Columbia University, where her research seeded a $1.2M federal grant.

Now she is building the biggest version of that pattern. Her research on co-adaptive human-AI collaboration is under review at the International Journal of Human-Computer Interaction and AI & Society, and PinocchAIo is where it becomes a product: a licensable core with a market of every person and every industry that wants an AI of their own.
